Learning how to learn · 15 minutes

From memory to understanding

Practice retrieving an idea, then use feedback to improve your explanation.

By the end, explain the difference between recognition and retrieval, and design a short retrieval practice.

Example lesson shell. Authors replace the content sections and embedded lesson data together. Your answers remain in this tab until you download them; refreshing closes that unsaved work.

01 / Before reading

Start with what you remember

Without checking notes, put your current understanding into words. Uncertainty is useful evidence.

02 / Core idea

Familiarity is a starting point

Recognition happens when an idea looks familiar with the source in front of you. Retrieval asks you to produce that idea when the source is absent. Those are different tasks.

A practical loop

Close the source. Explain the idea. Compare your explanation with the source. Correct one specific gap. Try again later.

03 / Visual & data slot

Make the difference visible

04 / Case slot

A familiar page, a missing explanation

Alex rereads a page and recognizes every sentence. In a meeting the next day, Alex cannot explain the central idea without opening the page.

Which practice would expose that gap before the meeting? Consider what evidence each activity produces.
