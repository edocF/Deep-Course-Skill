# Research and sources

Read for broad curriculum research, focused lesson research, real cases or data,
changing requirements, or unavailable/conflicting evidence. Keep domain findings
under the selected course's research/ directory; keep lesson claim support in its
sources.md. The director uses available Codex research tools, without a separate
model service or bundled subject pack.

## Research to the decision

During creation, establish the domain map, prerequisite structure, source classes,
and unresolved questions needed to justify the proposal. For a lesson, investigate
the chosen objectives, examples, misconceptions, and data rather than generating
the whole course. Prefer original research, official documentation, standards
owners, public agencies, original datasets, and direct records. Use secondary
explanations to locate or interpret primary material; distinguish interpretation
from the underlying finding. Read the supporting passage, not just search snippets.

Cross-check consequential, disputed, surprising, or numerical claims against an
independent authoritative source or reproducible calculation. A second page
copying the same original claim is not independent confirmation. For conflicting
results, check definitions, population, measurement method, version, and date;
show the remaining disagreement and how it affects the activity. If a controlling
source is unique, identify it and the limit on corroboration.

## Keep claims traceable

For every sourced claim or dataset, sources.md records the claim ID, supported
statement or data fields, source title, author/publisher, URL or stable local
identifier, and page/section/table when available. Include publication date or
version; add an access date for online/current resources or changing documents.
Reference the claim ID or source link beside the lesson assertion and distinguish
quoted text, paraphrase, and the lesson author's inference. For local sources,
use a resolvable identifier and explain access limits; never imply public access.

For example, a real-case data row needs a source entry naming the table and date,
units, and population. Its computed comparison also exposes the inputs, formula,
method, and rounding so a learner can reproduce it. A constructed example instead
labels its data as simulated and states its assumptions; it does not masquerade
as a sourced real event.

Verify time-sensitive facts at lesson preparation: state the relevant as-of date,
jurisdiction, version, units, and applicability. A retrieval date does not prove a
document is current. Trace revised or superseded requirements to their controlling
version. Keep fetched data and calculations inspectable; use exact values from
the cited dataset rather than plausible invented values. Generated images can
illustrate an idea but cannot establish real-world events or precise measurements.

Stable mathematical derivations, ordinary explanations, and clearly constructed
practice problems need no decorative citation. Their assumptions and calculations
must still be inspectable. Source specific empirical findings, statistics, real
cases, standards, thresholds, intervals, and contested or changing claims. Follow
applicable research requirements when the topic calls for verification.

## When evidence is unavailable

Identify the exact unavailable source or unverified claim and its consequence.
Continue with stable explanation, a labeled simulation, or another accessible
authoritative source when that preserves the objective. Withhold unsupported
operational values and conclusions that require current verification. Mark the
claim as unresolved in research and sources.md; report the date and scope of any
older evidence used. Do not fabricate a citation, quote, access date, or check.

Research for the current operation is complete when every material sourced claim
has traceable support or an explicit unresolved status, calculations reproduce,
and uncertainty is visible where it changes the learner's conclusion. A missing
essential source leaves the dependent activity pending; available independent
teaching can still proceed.
